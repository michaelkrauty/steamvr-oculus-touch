# Changelog

All notable user-facing changes are recorded here. Version numbers follow semantic versioning; changes before 1.0 may alter interfaces.

## Unreleased

### Added

- When a controller's radio goes silent while it is in use (it turned in the 3 s before), or comes back after 0.5 s or more within the 10 s it is held, the daemon saves what the radio delivered from 20 s before its last report to 10 s after, and the haptic commands sent meanwhile, as a recording in the state directory (`dropout-<unix time>-<hand>.rec`, the newest ten kept, on a thread of its own), and logs where; a gap under 0.5 s that ends is a blip and is not recorded. It keeps the radio's last minute in memory for this, in a fixed ring the radio thread copies each report into without ever waiting: a report that arrives while a recording is being copied out is left out of the ring. `cv1touch report` sends the newest three; they hold no camera images. Recordings (`cv1touch record`) now also hold the haptic commands the daemon got (record type 8).
- The headset alignment measures where the base station SteamVR tracked the headset from is in the cv1touch world. Captures record, from SteamVR's log, the station each Lighthouse device was tracked from throughout (`tracking_bases` in the truth file), and the registration keeps that station's world pose along with those earlier alignments against the same calibration measured; one SteamVR moved during the capture is not measured, an alignment that measures no station keeps none, `register --stations` and `calibrate rig` clear them, and the levelling of `calibrate gravity` turns them with the world. The daemon follows a measured station as closely as the alignment measured it. A station no alignment measured is placed by the mean over SteamVR's settled layouts of where each puts it relative to the measured stations: on one setup a single layout puts a station 2 to 17 cm off over the play area, and the mean of seven about 1 cm. While the history holds fewer than eight layouts, an alignment adds those its earlier captures saw. `cv1touch register --follow` takes `--tracking-base LHB-SERIAL` for a capture made before captures recorded the station.
- `cv1touch setup --redo STAGE` offers a finished stage again, with redo and keep (`sensors`, `floor`, `model`, `buttons` or `headset`), for redoing one step with the guide after something changed that the guide cannot see, such as a new SteamVR room setup; its saved result stays until the step is redone, and quitting at the prompt changes nothing.
- A sensor that is moved, bumped or re-mounted is recalibrated by the daemon itself while the controllers are tracked, with nothing to do, as long as two or more sensors stayed put: each sensor's frames are checked against the pose the others set (a controller nearer than 0.6 m is no check), one that disagrees for two windows of 5 s stops being tracked with, its frames are gathered as probes (the controller as the others track it, with the LEDs the engine labels), and a pose placed from them and confirmed by a second batch within 10 mm and 0.5 degrees is tracked with at once, given to the engine, and written into the calibration (the old file kept as a backup; the sensor's landmarks are dropped). `cv1touch status --json` shows each sensor's `pose` state and `sensors_unsure`; `cv1touch doctor` gets a `sensor_poses` check. A pose placed within 3 mm and 0.2 degrees of the calibrated one is a false alarm: nothing is written and the sensor is tracked with again; so is a sensor that sees no labelled controller for 30 s or gets no pose in 3 minutes, and a sensor placed three times in half an hour is given up on. The calibration file is written only while `cv1touch setup` is not running and only when it is still the file the daemon loaded; the stages `setup` had completed stay completed, and of the backups the daemon's own writes make only the newest ten are kept (other tools' backups are never touched). With fewer than two agreeing sensors, or no pose that holds up after six batches, the daemon says to run `cv1touch setup`.
- `cv1touchd --supervise` runs the daemon as a child process with each line of its output stamped with the time in `logs/cv1touchd.log` under the state directory, keeping four earlier logs. After a failure it starts the daemon again 2 s later, as the systemd unit does, unless the daemon refused its arguments or failed five times in a row within 30 s of starting. It is meant for Windows, where no service manager does this.
- A Windows installer, `cv1touch-setup-<version>.exe` (Inno Setup; built by CI from the Windows bundle `windows\bundle.ps1` collects). It installs for the person running it, with no administrator rights, gives the sensors their driver (`cv1touch sensors install`, one permission prompt), registers the SteamVR driver and starts the daemon, which from then on starts at logon without a window through the new `cv1touchd-quiet`. A step that does not finish (no sensor plugged in, SteamVR running or missing) is said at the end, and the Start menu's Finish setting up runs both again; the Start menu also gets Set up, Check, Send a report, Start and Stop. The uninstaller stops the daemon, unregisters the driver and takes the sensors' driver off; settings and calibration stay. The bundle carries the terms of every library the Windows build links in (`third_party\licenses`, see `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`). `docs/windows.md` tells a tester what to do.
- `cv1touch sensors` says whether each Rift sensor plugged in can be used: on Linux, that the udev rules let it be opened. On Windows, where the engine reads the sensors through WinUSB and Windows gives them no driver of its own, `cv1touch sensors install` gives each sensor's two interfaces Windows' own WinUSB driver (the Microsoft-signed "WinUsb Device", as choosing it in Device Manager does; no other driver or certificate is installed), asking Windows for permission once, and `cv1touch sensors remove` takes off what it gave, leaving a WinUSB someone else set up alone. Both say so, and exit with code 3, when a program was using a sensor and Windows must be restarted to finish. `cv1touch doctor` checks it on Windows.
- `cv1touch report` saves one zip file with what someone helping you needs to see what went wrong: a summary (version, system, the `doctor` checks, the Oculus USB devices and the drivers they run with, the daemon's status and setup's state), the ends of the daemon's and SteamVR's logs, and cv1touch's settings and calibration. On Windows it goes on the desktop and Explorer shows it; elsewhere it goes in the current directory, or wherever `--out` says. It says what it holds: SteamVR's log also names your other VR devices, your user folder and your network addresses.
- `cv1touch stop` stops the daemon cleanly, as SIGTERM or Ctrl+C does, and with it a `cv1touchd --supervise` running it, and waits until both have ended (10 s at most; exit code 2 when one still runs), so that what comes next finds their files free. The supervisor itself stops the same way: Ctrl+C or SIGTERM asks the daemon to stop, and ends it if it has not stopped 10 s later. Closing the supervisor's console window leaves the daemon 5 s, as Windows then ends the supervisor, and logging off ends both at once; `cv1touch stop` is the clean way.
- `cv1touch tilt DEGREES` shows the controllers pitched from their tracked pose, positive with the forward end up, within +-45 degrees, for applications that hold a tool in the hand at a fixed angle and offer no way to change it (a gun pointing too low or too high for your grip). The pose SteamVR hands applications and the render model pitch together, applied at once, kept across daemon restarts in `settings.json` as `tilt_deg` and shown by `cv1touch status --json`; `cv1touch tilt 0` shows them as tracked again. Nothing about tracking changes: the render offsets `cv1touch fit-render` fitted are turned by that pitch when published.
- `cv1touch prediction [PERCENT]` sets how much of the tracked motion SteamVR extrapolates poses with: 100 (the default) as before, 0 none, so each pose is shown where it was measured. `cv1touch prediction position PERCENT` and `cv1touch prediction rotation PERCENT` set positions and orientations separately: positions as tracked keep the controllers with the body on a sidestep, while orientations shown where they were measured were the way around a tilt during fast turns (fixed below). Settings apply at once and are kept for later runs in `settings.json` in the configuration directory (`rotation_prediction_percent` for orientations; a file without it means the same for both). The driver applies them, whichever poses are published (also OpenHMD's): daemon and driver must be updated together (shared-memory ABI 4).

### Changed

- The latency adjustment, `cv1touch latency MS` (how much earlier than their measured time published poses are stamped, on top of the 4 ms the pipeline measured for the radio link; a negative value stamps them later), is kept across daemon restarts in `settings.json` as `latency_adjustment_ms`, shown by `cv1touch status --json` and logged when the daemon starts with one; before, it lasted until the daemon was next started. The reply says which way the stamps move. `cv1touch eval` says, under the lag it measures between the published stamps and the Lighthouse-tracked reference on the jig (negative: the Touch runs ahead of the reference), the `cv1touch latency` value that puts the Touch on the reference's timeline (captures record the adjustment they were made with, so the value is absolute; for older captures it says how much to add), and leaves a lag within 0.5 ms alone. Which timeline is the true one it cannot tell. The driver submits a pose stamped after the submission itself as current, so an adjustment that stamps poses later than their samples arrived (below about -4 ms) has no further effect.
- The SteamVR driver's log line on the age of the poses it submits (every 10 s in SteamVR's `vrserver.txt`) now also says how much of that age passed after the daemon published the pose, which separates the driver's own wake-up from everything before it (the radio link and the daemon).
- Controllers are tracked again sooner after waking up or losing their position. The tracker also starts from OpenHMD poses rated good, not only strong, and after a loss it searches for the controller's position itself with the orientation the gyro kept. A track started either way is shown only once a second sensor, or a strong OpenHMD pose that agrees with it, confirms it. Replaying recordings from 2026-10-08, wake-ups that had taken 6.6 s and 2.9 s took 0.24 s and 1.5 s and the other three 43-63% less time, with no new wrong locks. `cv1touch status --json` counts these acquisitions (`good`, `searched`, `unconfirmed`).
- `cv1touchd` no longer runs OpenHMD's brute-force pose search for a controller its own tracker fitted within the last 100 ms, or one asleep, except on a sensor that has no pose yet. That search took most of the daemon's CPU time during play. The per-sensor OpenHMD fix counts in `cv1touch status` are lower for tracked controllers as a result.

### Fixed

- Haptics and base-station reports reach the daemon when Linux SteamVR runs inside Steam's pressure-vessel container, which hides the usual control socket. The driver can also use a private socket beside the shared-memory poses and remembers which socket it reached, without retrying a command that may already have arrived. Both the daemon and driver need this update for container support; either still works with an older counterpart outside the container. Instances started with an explicit runtime directory or shared-memory name keep their own control endpoint. Pose transport and Windows are unchanged.
- Paced accuracy captures (`cv1touch eval-capture`) keep the base stations' poses alongside the selected Index controller, as free captures do, so a registration fitted with `register --follow` has stations for the daemon to follow when SteamVR changes its layout. Both capture modes timestamp each pose when it is read and check devices one index at a time, instead of pausing the pose stream for a full inventory scan. Poses are kept only after the next identity check confirms the device number still belongs to the same device; an interval whose reported identity or tracking validity changed is discarded. The timestamp and device-number fixes also repair free captures. The selected reference and truth-file format stay the same; older captures are unchanged.
- The daemon no longer runs out of file descriptors after many hours with SteamVR closed, leaving it unreachable and making `cv1touch doctor` fail its daemon check. The universe watch checks for SteamVR's server before connecting: failed OpenVR starts can leak descriptors inside SteamVR's library. Failed connections are retried after 60 s, then with the wait doubling to at most 15 minutes, even if driver reports arrive and go stale meanwhile. Process checks run only when a connection attempt is due; once the server is found stopped, they run at most every 10 s. A successful connection or a stop detected at the next check resets the backoff. `cv1touch universe-watch` shows whether it is waiting for SteamVR or retrying, and how long until the next check. The setup guide also checks that the server is running before each retry. A daemon that has already exhausted its descriptors still needs to be restarted.
- The daemon reports a sensor's landmarks moving once per alert, after two consecutive one-minute windows over their threshold, and recovery once the shift falls below that alert's threshold with at least as many landmarks as when it was raised. A persistent shift between 0.4 and 1 px repeated the recovery notice every minute, and losing landmarks could falsely announce recovery by switching to the higher single-landmark threshold. More landmarks may tighten an active alert's threshold; fewer cannot clear it. A window without landmarks breaks a pending movement streak and leaves an active alert alone. The threshold values and tracking are unchanged.
- A controller whose radio goes silent for a moment no longer drops out of the game. After 100 ms without a report the daemon told SteamVR that the controller had disconnected and released its inputs, so a game let go of whatever the hand held; on one setup the right Touch went silent three times within 11 s during a match, for 0.5 s, then 7 s, then for good. A controller whose reports stop now stays connected for up to 10 s, out of range, where it was, with every button, the trigger and grip held as they were when its reports stopped; when they resume it carries on, and after 10 s it is in standby and its inputs are released as before. The daemon logs when a controller's radio goes silent, whether it was in use then, and when it is back, and `cv1touch status` shows such a controller as held. The daemon and the driver must both be updated for the whole of it: an older driver lets go of the controller after 1 s.
- The controllers no longer sit centimetres off the headset after SteamVR re-solves its base-station layout, which it does at nearly every start. SteamVR tracks the headset in the frame of the base station it bootstrapped from and re-solves the stations' poses relative to each other, by different amounts each time (one station by 110 and then 120 mm within a minute on one setup), while the stations stay where they are mounted. The universe watch followed the stations' moves together, so after a re-solve the controllers sat where no single station put them: on one setup's headset-alignment captures under four layouts it predicted each capture's registration from another's to 5 to 82 mm, against 13 to 134 mm without following. It now follows only the station SteamVR tracks the headset from, which SteamVR's log names (`vrserver.txt`, found through OpenVR's path registry), from where that station is in the cv1touch world; on the same captures, with each station placed by other captures, that predicts each to 2 to 7.5 mm. Through a moment without tracking it keeps to the last station named, and without the log it follows the stations' moves together as before. A registration made before this places each station where the layout it was fitted in puts it, which is right for the station SteamVR tracked the headset from at the time. The daemon also keeps each layout SteamVR settles on (one it keeps for two minutes) in `station-layouts.json` in the state directory. `cv1touch universe` says which station SteamVR tracks the headset from, how each station's place is known and how far SteamVR's layout now puts what each station tracks from where the headset's frame has it; `cv1touch universe-watch` says what the registration follows.
- `cv1touch register --follow` fits a capture made before the calibration was last written, which the daemon's own re-placing of a bumped sensor makes of every earlier capture. The fit replayed such a capture under the installed calibration, and the replay has nothing to start from when the daemon tracked throughout, so it failed with "never moved with the reference". When the replay tracks less than half of what the daemon published, the published poses are now carried into the installed calibration's world through the change of frame the sensors show; a sensor the newer calibration moved by more than 10 mm or 1 degree against the others is left out when three or more remain, and otherwise the capture is refused.
- The headset alignment's nod-and-tilt step no longer ends after the first tilt. It ended once the head's pitch and roll had each covered 30 degrees of range, which the circles' wobble plus one tilt toward one shoulder could satisfy; it now needs a nod up and a nod down and a tilt toward each shoulder, each at least 15 degrees from level, as the instructions say.
- Headset alignment (`cv1touch setup`, `cv1touch register --headset`, `register --follow`) no longer fails with "never moved with the reference" on a good capture. The fit replayed the capture through a fresh tracker, which has had no engine poses to start from since the daemon stopped running the engine's search for a controller it tracks, so it never acquired either Touch. The fit now uses the poses the daemon published during the capture, unless the calibration was written after the capture (then it is replayed as before, and the fit says so). `register --headset` also says that the recording runs from the start and keeps only the rigid stretch, so there is no need to be holding the controllers to the headset when the command starts.
- The controller models sit on the real controllers without a constant tilt. `cv1touch fit-render` (the controller-model stage of `cv1touch setup`) fitted the factory LED positions onto the SteamVR body mesh's surface by nearest point; the LEDs sit under a cover, and near the ring's edges the nearest surface is the ring's side wall rather than the LED's window, so the fit pulled one side of the ring in and the other out (under the previous offsets the LEDs lay from 2 mm inside to 1.3 mm outside the surface around the ring, and five per hand faced 60 to 140 degrees away from the surface they were matched to), a few degrees of pitch and a mirrored roll between the hands. Each LED is now matched to the window its emission ray leaves the mesh through, and all LEDs sit a common depth under their windows, fitted along with the pose. An LED whose ray leaves the mesh through a face that does not face it (more than 30 degrees off) is under no window the mesh models and is left out: the two LEDs on the handle leave the body mesh through faces 34 to 50 degrees off, 3 to 5 mm in front of them, and matched to those faces they pulled the fit 4.5 degrees off the ring (3 degrees of pitch and a 2-degree roll mirrored between the hands); an LED whose ray leaves through no face is held under the nearest surface until the pose brings it back under its window, and one still without a window once the others sit under theirs points out of a gap in the mesh and is left out too. With fewer than six LEDs under windows nothing is installed. The ring's 22 LEDs then sit a common depth under their windows to 0.04 mm, and the two hands' fits agree to 0.2 degrees. The report says how many LEDs sit under windows the mesh models, that depth and how far the LEDs depart from it. Rerun `cv1touch fit-render` (or the setup stage) to take it.
- Rotation prediction no longer tilts a controller during fast turns. SteamVR integrates a driver's angular velocity in the device's own frame, not in the world's as its documentation says (measured with a synthetic pose: a rate about the world's up axis was applied about the pitched controller's own axis, whatever the alignment or render offset), and the driver gave it the world rate, so every predicted turn went about an axis off by the controller's orientation, more the faster the turn. The driver now gives SteamVR the body rate. `cv1touch prediction rotation 100` is worth trying again where it was turned down for the tilt.
- A controller that has just woken up, or whose wrong lock was just dropped, no longer appears at OpenHMD's own position estimate (which could be metres off) until it is tracked again: SteamVR shows it where it was last tracked, turning but not moving. The daemon log now says how long each controller went without a position when it gets one back, and logs that after a wake too.

## [0.2.0](https://github.com/michaelkrauty/steamvr-oculus-touch/releases/tag/v0.2.0) - 2026-10-11

### Added

- `cv1touch setup` guide for camera, gravity, render, input, and headset-registration stages, with a private resumable journal and dependency-aware reuse.
- Read-only `setup --check` and `setup --check --json` reports; explicit visual-verification/adoption of legacy calibration without discarding its files.
- Controller-button and haptic checks before headset registration, with a button-started haptic countdown and B on the right controller to cancel the capture.
- Headset registration ends its recording by itself once the person has turned, nodded and tilted enough, and stops it with three quick haptic pulses when a controller turns on the headset by more than about 3 degrees within a second (or on any other problem); the retry prompt, or `setup --no-slip-check`, turns the slip check off.
- `cv1touchd` status names its shared-memory segment (`shm`).

### Changed

- Guided input calibration captures both hands in four shared phases, reducing timed capture to 16 seconds while retaining freshness, identity, travel, and save/reload checks.
- An accepted fresh guided camera solve completes gravity alignment from the same motion capture; separate still-hold gravity refinement remains available.
- Guided input calibration precedes headset registration, leaving headset movement as the final physical setup step.
- The setup guide reads like a checklist: plain stage names (sensor positions, floor level, controller model, buttons and sticks, headset alignment), checks explained only when they fail, Enter/`q` (and `k` to keep unverified saved settings) instead of typed words, one result line per stage with solver output in a log, and an immediate retry after a failure. Captures start with A on the right controller as well as Enter, timed steps are marked by a controller haptic pulse and a speaker beep, and input calibration records each step as soon as both hands are in position. Instructions say what each step is for and exactly how to do it, and failures say in plain words what happened and that the previous calibration is unchanged.

### Fixed

- Failed camera captures recover provisional tracking when the previous calibration is malformed; rejected reloads attempt recovery and report failure.
- Cancelled render, registration, gravity, placement, and refinement fits do not install their computed settings.
- Guided registration bootstraps a fresh fit after camera-world changes instead of depending on an obsolete saved transform.
- Guided camera/gravity handoffs wait for the requested engine configuration, advancing sensor streams, and fresh data from the same controllers before continuing.
- Pending setup stages retain missing or invalid artifact diagnostics instead of offering adoption of unavailable settings.
- Guided headset registration explains preparation before recording and waits for a deliberate button press and haptic countdown, so users can get into position without watching the terminal.
- Guided capture validates recording acknowledgements and scopes cleanup to its own recording when a start reply is lost.
- Guided calibration prompts identify buttons by controller, state when input sampling begins, and separate the control checks from headset instructions.
- A controller falling asleep before the registration countdown (during the button checks or while reading the headset instructions) no longer aborts setup: the guide names the controller to wake, waits up to five minutes, and then needs a fresh release and press. A dropout during the countdown or recording still cancels without installing a fit.

## [0.1.0](https://github.com/michaelkrauty/steamvr-oculus-touch/releases/tag/v0.1.0) - 2026-10-10

### Added

- Complete Linux CV1 Touch stack: Rust daemon/tracker, camera calibration, SteamVR-space registration, native SteamVR driver, haptics, recording and replay.
- User-local installer, service setup, USB/hidraw access rules, documented setup and expert paths.
- Pinned OpenVR SDK, attributed modified OpenHMD source, build and test automation.
- Dynamic sensor discovery, calibration, tracking, and complete diagnostics without a fixed camera-count cap.
- Guided per-controller input calibration for trigger/grip endpoints and thumbstick centers/full travel, with live reload and factory reset.

### Changed

- Source documentation and recording diagnostics describe current behavior without unpublished development notes or phase labels.
- Original-code copyright notices and software citation identify the maintainer by GitHub username.
- Upstream Linux CI runs maintainer changes automatically and requires explicit workflow approval for external fork revisions on the dedicated runner; manual branch checks and independent GitHub-hosted checks in forks are available.
- Project and repository renamed to SteamVR Oculus Touch (`steamvr-oculus-touch`); command names and configuration paths remain compatible.

### Fixed

- Registered SteamVR driver upgrades and removal now preserve modified or unmanaged files; verified ownership metadata and atomic package replacement protect installation changes.
- Installation and runtime dependencies on a developer's checkout and CPU topology.
- First-time headset registration without an existing alignment file.
- Concurrent alignment writes, stale controller inputs, and SIGPIPE on driver control sends.
- USB camera shutdown deadlock that could stall daemon restarts.
- Multi-camera teardown blocked by a peer's USB retry loop; fully parked streams now recover through bounded engine-side retries.
- Camera allocation/startup failure cleanup and frame-buffer lifetime during shutdown.
- Fixed-size cross-camera state, high-index camera aliasing, and camera queue starvation under load.
- Out-of-range analog inputs after factory calibration drift; SteamVR output now stays within its normalized ranges.
- Input diagnostics accepting unrelated axes or missing updates during driver delivery lag.
- Linux OpenVR controller-state packing that could prevent input diagnostics from reading SteamVR buttons and axes correctly.
