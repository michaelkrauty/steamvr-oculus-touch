# Landmark displacement watch

The daemon watches learned static infrared emitters in one-minute windows, even when controllers sleep. It measures one common displacement, up to 2 pixels, independently of the 0.8 pixel radius used to learn a mode. Learned modes can be only 1.6 pixels apart, so this range overlaps neighbouring modes. Widening a nearest-neighbour radius alone would confuse those modes. Two consecutive windows above 0.4 pixels with two or more landmarks, or 1 pixel with a single landmark, raise an alert. Unknown windows clear the published displacement and interrupt a pending streak, but never recover an active alert. Tracking and automatic sensor placement do not use this watch.

A measurement needs at least two coherent spots with unique matches, except for a sensor with only one learned landmark. A completely hidden, separate group need not match. Every supporting observed spot must have exactly one coherent explanation using learned emitters across **all translations**, including translations beyond the 2 pixel reporting range. Explanations may assign two spots to the same emitter: a single genuine source can split into recurring modes. Any explanation different from the proposed fit makes the result unknown. The proposed fit still uses distinct observed spots and learned emitters. A partial repeated pair cannot be disambiguated by an absent third emitter; an observed third emitter can disambiguate it. Fully observed repeated patterns can still be measured.

The guarantee is conditional: **a published displacement is within 0.4 pixels of the true displacement, up to numerical roundoff, if each supporting spot is an observation of a genuine learned emitter and its centroid error is at most 0.4 pixels**. Two supporting spots may come from one emitter; distinct emitters are not an assumption of the guarantee. This is an assumed bound on each extracted mode's centroid error, not on every raw sample and not a confidence interval inferred from measured scatter. Spot scatter (clamped to 0.12–0.4 pixels) also controls the stricter fit-quality checks. Persistent spurious infrared sources are allowed, provided they do not counterfeit a learned pattern: reproduce, within scatter, the relative geometry of two or more learned emitters at another translation. A counterfeit is the case the watch cannot detect from pixels alone. A lone learned emitter has no relative geometry to check and relies entirely on genuine support. In particular, the same collected pixel can mean a small genuine move or a large move with the original emitter outside collection and a spurious source left in its place.

The uniqueness check retains every correspondence whose displacement vectors obey the necessary pairwise error bound of 0.8 pixels, including repeated learned-emitter indices. Thus two nearby modes of one emitter cannot masquerade as two independent landmarks. Another observed emitter can rule out a repeated-emitter explanation, allowing a measurement even with close supporting spots; a blanket rejection of close pairs would lose that evidence. Proof-critical comparisons add an absolute margin of 1e-12 pixels plus 128 machine epsilons times the largest original coordinate magnitude (at least one). Scaling from original coordinates covers precision lost when subtracting large coordinates, even if the resulting displacement is small. This is conservative: extra retained alternatives can only make the result unknown. Some retained alternatives might fail a tighter fit, so uncertain cases remain unknown. The true correspondence is always among them under the stated centroid bound, by the triangle inequality. If only one correspondence survives, it must be the true one; averaging its errors preserves the 0.4 pixel bound. The search never uses absence, a predicted footprint, or a minimum-hit decision to refute an alternative. Consequently, clipping and thinning can remove positive evidence and produce unknown, but cannot make a false correspondence unique. A bounded search that exhausts its node budget also returns unknown.

The radio thread collects samples into two preallocated buffers per watched sensor. Each buffer holds at most 65,536 samples (1 MiB). A normal-priority background worker extracts modes and searches correspondences after a window closes. At most one job per sensor is in flight. A busy worker or an overflowing buffer makes the new window unknown; it supersedes older results. Radio-side exchanges never wait for a lock. Completed buffers are recycled separately from publication, and overdue window sequences are advanced before results can be accepted. An older recovery cannot clear an alert before a newer unknown window is applied. A completed window superseded before publication is reported as unknown, interrupting any pending movement streak even if its buffer was already recycled. Publication uses the radio loop's current clock, not a delayed frame's capture timestamp. Results are consumed even without new camera frames, and results from an older reference epoch are discarded after a pose change. A panic inside one analysis is contained: that window is unknown, the worker carries on with the next, and the daemon logs one line.

The deterministic property suite covers bounded centroid errors, single genuine emitters alternating between modes with 0.4 pixel RMS sample scatter, repeated groups, translations through 20 pixels, collection clipping, thinning below the minimum hit count, and persistent non-counterfeiting spurious sources. The split-mode families include clipping, below-minimum hit counts and several genuine emitters, with symmetric scatter that keeps the collected mode centroids bounded even though raw samples can exceed the centroid bound. Its independent scene generator rejects counterfeit geometry before running the matcher; it never filters scenes based on matcher output. The clipping regressions are included explicitly, along with numerical boundary scenes: errors exactly at 0.4 pixels or one ULP below it, coordinates through a billion pixels, and large translations. Dedicated tests cover 65-reference rejection and node-budget exhaustion after a first candidate was found with an alternative unexplored. Larger offline runs use `CV1TOUCH_LANDMARK_PROPERTY_SCENES` (up to 200,000 scenes); the normal suite runs 256 scenes.
