# Architecture

```text
CV1 headset radio / USB sensors
          │
          ▼
Modified OpenHMD acquisition engine (C/C++)
  radio reports, IMU, factory calibration, images, blobs
          │
          ▼
cv1touchd (Rust)
  optical association + error-state Kalman filter
  bias estimation, recovery, prediction shaping
          │
          ├── local control socket / recording and replay
          ▼
Versioned shared memory (seqlocks + futex notification)
          │
          ▼
Native SteamVR driver (C++)
  poses, inputs, capacitive touch, battery, haptics
```

OpenHMD owns device discovery and USB/camera acquisition. The Rust tracker fuses gyroscope and accelerometer data with multi-camera LED observations; OpenHMD poses provide acquisition priors/fallback. A pose it rates strong starts a published track at once; one it rates only good starts a provisional track, shown only once a second sensor's frame (or a strong pose) confirms it. After a loss, while the gyro still holds the controller's orientation, the tracker also searches for its position by itself (`track::search`), with a bounded cost per frame, and treats what it finds as provisional too. The headset is used as a radio receiver and does not become a SteamVR display device. The driver is deliberately thin and shares the C ABI in `common/cv1touch_shm.h` with the daemon.

## Coordinates and timestamps

Factory LED geometry, per-camera optical coordinates, the calibrated tracking world, the SteamVR tracking universe, and the rendered controller model are different frames. Camera calibration supplies world-from-camera transforms; registration supplies SteamVR-from-world; render fitting supplies the controller-model offset. SteamVR tracks each Lighthouse device in the frame of the base station it bootstrapped from, and re-solves the stations' poses relative to each other, differently each session (the device moves with its own base when that is the station moved); so a registration measures where that one station is in the world, and the daemon follows the station SteamVR tracks the headset from, which only SteamVR's log names (`steamvr_log`, `align::Registration::station_map`). This was established in 2026-10 from headset-alignment captures under four layouts; check it again when SteamVR changes. Orientation serialization uses XYZW quaternions. See the ABI headers and `src/calib` for units and transform direction.

Host/device clock mapping and exposure-time compensation place measurements on a monotonic timeline. Published poses carry their sample time and velocities. The driver reports pose age to SteamVR, which performs its own prediction with the share of those velocities `cv1touch prediction` sets (positions and orientations separately). SteamVR integrates the angular velocity a driver reports in the frame of the reported orientation, not in driver world space as its driver documentation says, and takes the linear velocity in driver world space, rotated by the world-from-driver alignment; the driver therefore hands over the body rate (`ang_vel - gyro_bias`) and the world velocity. This was measured in 2026-10 with `examples/steamvr_prediction_probe.rs`, which publishes a synthetic controller and reads back what SteamVR predicts; run it again when SteamVR changes. Published stamps are checked against a Lighthouse-tracked controller fixed to the same jig by `cv1touch eval-capture` and `cv1touch eval`, which reports the lag between the two (negative: the Touch ahead) and the `cv1touch latency` adjustment that puts the Touch on the reference's timeline; the driver submits a pose stamped after the submission itself as current, so stamping later than the samples' arrival has no further effect; the daemon keeps that adjustment in `settings.json` with the prediction percents. Software delivery time, hardware radio cadence, optical exposure age, and display latency must not be combined into one misleading latency number.

## Reliability boundaries

Shared-memory slots use single-writer seqlocks. Alignment writers are serialized; hand ownership transfers between radio and fallback publishing. Driver readers reject incompatible mappings and stale heartbeats. A controller whose samples stop for over 100 ms is published as it was at its last sample, inputs held, out of range and still, for up to 10 s (a radio gap, `CV1T_FLAG_RADIO_GAP`), so a radio that drops it for a moment takes nothing from the game; after that, or when the daemon stops publishing, its inputs are released. The hand's gap and last live state belong to the daemon, across engine reopens and changes of publishing thread. Before waiting for the radio thread to finish, publication passes to the main heartbeat, which publishes the held state throughout engine close/open and initialization and expires it at the original deadline; late worker publications are rejected under the hand lock. Only a worker that published that hand within 100 ms, or, given it before a sample of its own, looked after it, makes the heartbeat skip the hand. The heartbeat checks this without the hand's lock, so a lazy native update blocked before its first publication cannot strand the hold, and a worker busy with a hand never makes the main thread wait; a lock a failed worker poisoned leaves the exit to the next heartbeat. Hand notices are emitted after publishing and releasing the lock; expiry releases both hands before its notices can wait on the log. A gap keeps what began it (radio silence, an engine reopen or a stalled publishing thread); the log names it, and only a radio gap can be saved as a dropout recording. A dropout request is not admission: the flight recorder acknowledges acceptance, or coverage by its pending window. A request beyond that window's cutoff stays in the hand's deferred queue until accepted, covered or cancelled; publication and the main save tick retry it under the existing hand lock, which the main thread only tries. Deferred requests coalesce windows and allocate only for an uncovered request while an older window awaits saving. A radio thread newly given a hand refreshes its persistent state even before it has a radio sample of its own, and starts a gap only once nothing has published the hand for 100 ms; the engine and the radio thread stamp their own reads of the reports, so a sample stamped before the newest one is the controller still reporting, not a gap. Stopping the daemon disconnects both hands before logging, draining control requests or waiting for the engine to close. Haptic/control sends use bounded local IPC and cannot deliver SIGPIPE to SteamVR. A separate daemon keeps device reconnects and tracking computation out of the SteamVR server.

A still gap whose start and sleep beacons were observed by a single reader is labelled asleep (`CV1T_FLAG_ASLEEP`, an additive flag); beacons never advance sample times and the full hold still applies. `cv1touch status` shows the flag; the driver ignores it, so SteamVR's log says `radio silent: held as it was` for a nap too. A still gap waits up to 1.25 s after its last sample for that evidence before its first radio-silence log; a gap begun in use logs at once. A still gap that ends within that wait logs its return only if it is recorded, so a blip leaves no unpaired line. Such sleeps are not dropout recordings.

The engine and the radio thread read the radio independently. Each passes every Touch message it decodes, a sample with its calibrated motion or an empty sleep beacon, through a fixed queue per hand without allocation or locks. Every sample is classified, whether or not its pose is published. A gap that begins within 3 s of motion is in use: it asks for a recording at its start, even if it expires or never returns, and is never a nap. A return after expiry does not request that same in-use window again. A still gap is a nap only when a single reader saw it begin and then received its beacons before its first return. A gap spanning a reader handoff is never proven a nap, even if ownership returns to the original reader.

A publisher change is decided once. The outgoing reader finishes its batch before releasing ownership. Both acquisition threads timestamp reports with the process's shared host-monotonic clock. The original last-sample anchor, motion time and gap lifecycle cross readers as absolute times; silence is always the current receive or tick time minus that anchor. Publication never pairs an earlier silence measurement with a later stamp, so a scheduling pause cannot shorten a gap. Sleep proof and asleep labels stay with their reader. Receiver-only motion is evaluated relative to the actual gap start, not takeover. A non-publisher retains a bounded interval of its own motion times: a later moving return cannot erase earlier use. If that interval intersects the 3 s before the inherited start, use is kept conservatively. An open Radio gap promoted to in-use explicitly asks for capture with its original anchor and clears asleep. Reopen and Stall gaps never arm a capture, including promotion and unknown returns.

Imported in-use remains a plain boolean. It holds for a full 3 s from the actual takeover timestamp, read on the shared host clock when history is inherited, conservatively over-approximating the source's motion window. An earlier loop tick cannot start that window. A receiver-local still sample does not clear it. Every gap whose last sample falls inside that window follows the in-use rule, even if it never returns; after the window, only receiver-local evidence establishes use. The transferred motion time establishes use for the inherited gap; source age never shortens the imported boolean's window.

A still handoff-spanning gap records a first return at least 0.5 s after its last sample, even after the 10 s hold, and also records an unknown or lost first return. Either is standby from `flight::LATEST_NS` (35.5 s) after the last sample: a window asked for then is due at once, and the flight ring, 32,768 records or 65.5 s of the radio's 500 reports a second, must still reach back 20 s before that sample; the 10 s it keeps to spare cover nearly 90 haptic commands a second sharing the ring. An ordinary still gap without sleep proof records a known return only at least 0.5 s after its last sample and before 10 s; known later returns are standby. Known shorter blips cancel only their own request. Any return either reader observed ends its Radio gap, including a discarded return before takeover; a loss bound that could lie inside it is an unknown return and cannot wait for a later sample to trigger recording. A sample received before or exactly at takeover establishes another handoff-spanning interval, anchored at that sample; only a sample received strictly after takeover can establish uninterrupted ownership. Overflow vetoes sleep and preserves motion conservatively; losing only beacons does not relax an ordinary gap's standby boundary. Several returns read before a publication each end their own gap. Whether an identity has requested capture stays with it through handoffs and expiry, independent of whether the writer has saved it or a publisher has ticked the expiry deadline. A recordable return keeps that request without arming it again. Returns and reopens cannot discard a deferred request; callbacks gain no allocation or lock.

A non-publishing reader discards its activity as it goes, retaining only its local timing for takeover, so stale overflow cannot become new evidence. Both readers read every report and stamp it at their own read, so a receiver's copy of an inherited interval's last sample can be stamped either side of the outgoing reader's anchor, by the difference in their read latencies. That copy never ends the interval: once the interval has opened (a nap settled for a reopen has), a gap began only after the connect timeout (100 ms) of silence, so a receiver's sample less than that after the anchor is a copy of the anchor's report or an earlier one, whether it was discarded, retained or lost to overflow, and its motion counts as motion before the gap. A copy read before the interval opened is the receiver's own stamp of the last sample and becomes the anchor. This relies on the two readers reading a report within 100 ms of each other; a genuine return the outgoing reader had not read yet, within 100 ms of its anchor, merges with the following silence, which can only add a recording. An engine session boundary is not itself a radio dropout. After both readers stop, their queued and discarded timing and motion are classified before the acquisition evidence is reset, unless a worker failed: its panic may have left that state half-updated, so teardown leaves it to the process exit. An existing Radio gap keeps its cause, absolute anchor, expiry and capture-requested state; a settled nap verdict is preserved separately, so reopening during a nap does not create a recording. New-reader beacons cannot prove an older unconfirmed gap asleep. A contradictory discarded return or lost activity vetoes the preserved nap conservatively. Pre-handoff classification and actual publication share the same freshness/cause decision, using a clock read after their locks. A missing-publication cause is established before draining activity. Pre-handoff snapshots are unpublished activity and cannot end a native hold. A native Reopen/Stall hold ends only at a fresh accepted publication; queued raw returns cannot log a wake during teardown or blocked tracker work. A raw return just before teardown may end a Radio gap, but its unpublished successor starts a native hold with the last published pose's original deadline. The heartbeat never takes acquisition ownership, drains an acquisition queue or starts the next reader's imported-use window. A stalled worker may still be producing: queued activity is consumed by its own reader, or after both readers have joined. Pose and input holding keep their own timestamps and 10 s limit.

`tests/gap_model.rs` checks seeded streams against a ground-truth oracle: 256 seeds per family normally, more with `CV1TOUCH_MODEL_SEEDS`. Each reader has its own read latency, 0 to 90 ms, so the two readers' stamps of one report differ in either order. Families cover ordered drains, handoffs, overflows, returns either side of 10 s and of the flight ring's 35.5 s limit, asymmetric receiver evidence without later samples, imported-use boundaries with intervening still samples, publication/takeover pauses with pre-takeover successor gaps, reopens during naps, Radio dropouts and native holds (including the 90/105 ms boundary and lost returns), and unpublished native drains across handoffs and overflows. The oracle never derives expectations from implementation history. It requires every real dropout's request and rejects nap classification of handoff-spanning gaps. Capture/cancel accounting is per original anchor, so one gap's blip cannot cancel another's recording. A separate seeded daemon test drives the real recorder with delayed save ticks and a ground-truth queue of recording windows (64 traces normally, more with `CV1TOUCH_CAPTURE_SEEDS`).

Optional [input calibration](input-calibration.md) maps measured trigger/grip endpoints and stick travel before publication. The wizard samples factory-normalized inputs before user correction, so repeated calibration does not compound corrections. A stable factory-calibration identity binds each hand's settings to its controller; mismatches use finite, bounded factory values. Live reload validates the complete configuration before replacing it under the publication lock. Disconnect release bypasses the transform, since a raw zero can represent a partial press after endpoint correction.

The daemon attempts bounded real-time priority through rtkit for selected transport threads and falls back with a diagnostic if policy denies it. By default it inherits the process's allowed CPUs. `CV1TOUCH_CPUS` is an optional explicit CPU-list override; invalid or unavailable CPUs are rejected rather than applying a development-machine topology. Avoid changing scheduling policy without measuring it under your real workload.

## Expert use

Build with `cargo build --locked --release`; install without services using `python3 scripts/install.py --no-service`. Run `cv1touchd` directly as your desktop user. The service is just a wrapper around this daemon. Use `--help` for diagnostic commands, calibration alternatives, explicit recording files, offline `track` replay, and predictor controls.

Input injection is disabled by default. `test-inject on` permits bounded injection for ten minutes; `test-inject off` disarms it immediately. It is for integration tests and can affect applications currently receiving controller input. The socket and shared memory are local to the user's runtime environment; they are not network APIs.

The acquisition engine discovers sensors dynamically and supports one controller pair. Sensor identities and tracking state are dynamically sized; there is no fixed camera-count cap. Two pending frames per admitted camera and a bounded clock queue prevent a stalled consumer from accumulating unlimited work, with round-robin camera service and visible drop counters. Other hardware drivers remain in the attributed upstream source tree but are not built or offered as supported cv1touch hardware. Future changes to the ABI or recording schema must update compatibility checks, readers, tests, and the changelog together.

## Sensor diagnostics and compatibility

`cv1touch status --json` returns every admitted sensor in `sensors`, with `sensor_total` and camera queue/drop counters. The live status board uses this complete list. A once-per-second USB metadata scan notices Rift sensors by vendor/product IDs and serial, without opening them; on Windows it lists only the devices the USB bus enumerated. The real-time radio thread takes the scan's lock only to apply a removal, never for tracker work. Changes settle for one second. A running engine opens no new sensors: a new attachment, once Linux USB access or the Windows driver and interfaces are ready, waits for the next engine start (a daemon restart or a mode change), which tries to open it. The engine's retained last-start records also account for attachments discovered during open, including failed starts; discovery retains the attachment with each attempt, using native bus/address plus a pinned sysfs directory inode on Linux and an immutable instance/arrival snapshot captured before native enumeration on Windows. A completion metadata scan must match that identity; a serial alone cannot consume a replacement's attempt. One rule decides what the watcher can do to the engine's sensor objects. While the engine holds a live object for a serial, the serial is in use: nothing the watcher sees makes it pending, other than as opening while the engine opens. Only evidence about the device an object opened retires it: the object's own NO_DEVICE, or a change settled from USB listings read after the object started that rules that device out. A serial names one device, so the serial being gone rules out whichever device the object opened, and so does the departure of an attachment such a listing showed for the serial. The start record's own identity is not used for this: a Windows arrival snapshot can predate a replug, and a Linux pin can fail. Listings read after the start are the one engine_session reads after the open, and every watcher scan but the first applied once the engine's roster is known; scans run one at a time, and that first one may have been read before the starts. From `before_open` until then nothing settles: no departure is blamed on an object not yet known, and nothing logged or shown about a sensor the opening engine may use says what it will do with it (status shows such sensors as opening). Removal is therefore keyed by attachment: a start of an attachment other than the one that left is a new connection. The roster replaces what `before_open` expected the engine to try. A failed start spends an attachment's attempt only with its exact identity, as listed after the open or, unlisted, as the watcher knows it; a start record that names no attachment spends the attempt `before_open` expected, unless that listing shows another attachment. A sensor the roster does not account for waits for the next engine start, and the log says so. The listing read after the open also counts as a scan of each sensor it lacks, whether or not the engine started it: that sensor is not ready, its removal settles like any other, and nothing says it waits for the next engine start. A surprise removal reaches the object as NO_DEVICE through its video transfers: libusb completes them with that status when Linux usbfs reports the device gone, and on Windows for the device-not-connected and no-such-device errors (not yet observed on Windows hardware); a resubmission to a removed device fails the same way. Where none arrives, the watcher's evidence above still retires the object, except after a replug too quick for two scans to see its gap, before any listing read after the start had shown the sensor: that stream stays in use, sending no video, until the next engine start. Linux discovery opens the sysfs directory with `O_PATH|O_DIRECTORY|O_CLOEXEC` before USB open, validates bus/address relative to that pin and retains one descriptor per start record until replacement, forget or engine close. The pinned kernfs inode cannot be reused by a replacement at the same port, even when USB addresses wrap; matching has no time tolerance. The watcher reads the same directory inode through its temporary metadata-only pin. Neither opens a pin on the radio or tracker paths. Later arrivals stay pending. A definite native device-removal result, including a failed start whose object was freed, retires its attachment and allows one recovery at the next engine start, after USB readiness settles, even if the OS reused its attachment ID. Another such failure of the same attachment waits for a USB change. Other failed starts are not retried before the next engine start. Removing a sensor retires its ingress, tracker, inspection, placement and diagnostic state by serial without reopening the headset radio. Its identity notices still waiting are applied first, so an invalidation still retracts what its evidence supported. Retiring its native object stops its inspection or placement search at the next loop boundary, so the daemon frees the one search slot without waiting for a restored pose the engine is no longer asked to apply; a pose applied to it afterwards starts no new search. Native sensor objects remain until engine teardown because the acquisition threads and exposure queues share their lifetime. A detached serial stays excluded until the next engine open, even when reattached meanwhile; a sensor without a calibrated pose can record observations but does not contribute to the Rust pose estimate until calibrated. Failed USB opens/starts are logged, and stalled streams are visible as non-advancing frame counters.

Video diagnostics keep three producing stages separate. `sensors[].frames_received` counts complete payloads delivered before the analysis lock/queue, after defensive raw layout validation. USB3 CV1 sensors use raw images; USB2 sensors use JPEG in normal tracking, setup and calibration. `video_format` records that negotiated input as `raw` or `jpeg`. `frames_decoded` counts JPEG images only after successful decoding and exact packed-GRAY8 validation; it is `null` for raw video. Doctor, report and board rates select received progress for raw video and decoded progress for JPEG. Unknown format or unavailable decoding cannot be verified from received payloads. JPEG decoding can lag capture because of queued analysis, and a passing decode cannot detect every kind of JPEG corruption.

`frames_dropped` counts rejected image candidates at UVC assembly or defensive capture/decode/layout validation. Each identified candidate is counted once; malformed packets before an image boundary, buffer starvation and intentional analysis-queue eviction are excluded. It does not share the top-level recording `dropped` or `camera_queue` drop counters. These 64-bit video counters start at zero with a native device context; a decrease or a format/source change needs a fresh baseline. Never subtract dropped from received to estimate decoded progress. Missing quality records publish `null`, while supported zero is a real zero. `sensor_video_quality: true` advertises the additive JSON even with an empty sensor list. Native quality diagnostics use a separate 56-byte record; the existing 40-byte acquisition record and SHM ABI are unchanged.

The controller/alignment shared-memory ABI is version 4: the published velocities are the tracked ones, and the alignment block carries the prediction percents the driver applies. Its eight sensor slots are a legacy diagnostic prefix, not a tracking limit. `sensor_shm_count` and `sensor_shm_truncated` describe that prefix explicitly. Use control JSON for the full list; the status board warns when it must fall back to shared memory. Recording records already identify sensors individually by serial, so the recording schema is unchanged by it. Recordings also hold the haptic commands the daemon gets (record type 8) and, for checking sensors during play, the engine's controller identification notices, each engine open and each retirement of a sensor's identities (types 9 to 11); readers skip record types they do not know. The daemon keeps the radio's last minute of reports and haptic commands in a fixed ring (`flight`) and saves the stretch around a controller's dropout in use as a recording of its own. A report or haptic command left out because the ring is busy adds to an atomic total and keeps its timestamp in a separate fixed history. A saved recording counts the skips timestamped within its window, before its first record and after its last included. Every producer registers before it reads the clock for its record's time and retires after insertion or skip publication; the fallback engine's radio reports are stamped again as they reach the daemon, since the engine reads its time before the daemon can register. A recording reads the registered count under the ring lock, after copying the ring and reading the total, so a record stamped in its window but put in the ring after the copy, or a producer paused after a failed lock attempt before assigning a skip sequence, is visible to it. The count is exact or unknown, never low: it is unknown while a registered producer is incomplete or a skip's timestamp is still being written, once history the window needs has been overwritten, or if the total changed while it was counted. It goes in the save log and in the reserved final u64 of the version-2 header, u64::MAX meaning unknown; old files have zero there and older readers still read the records, and `rec-summary` shows the count. `src/flight_model.rs` checks every interleaving of two producers' atomic steps, from registration and clock read to insertion or skip publication, with one recording's count, including the failed-lock/pre-sequence interval. The same explorer rejects registration after the clock read, admission after a failed lock, and a writer count read after the scan. A due window the ring holds no record of is not saved: an empty file would only push out a recording that holds something, and the log says it was too old to record.

### Recording completion and disk space

A recording reserves its session under the control command lock before storage runs outside that lock, including opening a file. Shutdown can therefore see a pending start and cancel it before it opens its file. `record start PATH [frames]` still replies `ok` only after the new file's header has been written. A second recording command is refused while one is pending; a matching stop also marks that session to stop, including while its header is still being written, and asks the client to check status and retry for the completed outcome. Haptics, status and daemon stop remain available. The writer never holds the ring lock during storage operations.

`cv1touchd --record PATH` reserves its session before control starts serving, but opens the file and writes its header on the recorder writer. While storage is pending, status reports `starting`, other recording starts are refused, and status, haptics and daemon stop remain available. Hardware opens only after the header succeeds. An open/header/space-check failure remains fatal even if another client has acknowledged it: the startup wait checks its separate startup outcome every 10 ms, so tracking starts within that of the header being written, and refreshes the heartbeat every 200 ms. The writer logs its failure once before closing the file, including if close stalls; errors after startup succeeds still have the writer as their logging owner. Cleanup waits at most another 500 ms for recording storage, removes its control sockets, retires shared memory and exits non-zero. Those intervals exclude ordinary scheduling and IPC cleanup. A shutdown request during the open follows the same bounded cleanup and retains the pending header result through the final storage wait. After a shutdown request only cleanup consumes the pending header result. It retains the original session's final outcome through close, independently of any acknowledged outcome or replacement recording. It checks that result and the current final drain status; an open, header or sync failure during cleanup exits non-zero even if the failed session was already acknowledged and replaced. If storage is still pending when the wait ends, shutdown logs that the file may be incomplete; a requested shutdown without a reported storage error still exits zero.

`record stop` and `record stop-if PATH` stop accepting samples immediately and wait up to 30 s for the writer to drain and sync, outside the global control lock. Successful replies retain `ok records=... dropped=... write_errors=0 bytes=...` and `ok stopped records=...` respectively; an unrelated scoped stop still returns `ok not-matching`. A file still finishing returns `err` saying so: check status and repeat the stop when storage responds. A failed write or sync returns `err` with the reason. Automatic calibration, evaluation and setup captures use exact-path stops and cleanup, so an early-end error cannot make cleanup stop a different recording. A finishing session keeps ownership of its path and refuses a new start. The writer reports completed errors and early-end reasons once; shutdown adds only a separate incomplete-file warning if storage is still pending. Daemon shutdown also waits at most 500 ms for recording storage; a writer still blocked then cannot guarantee a completed file when the process exits.

Status retains `recording_status` for the current or most recent session: `state` (`idle`, `starting`, `recording`, `finishing`, `complete`, `failed`), `pending` (outcome not yet acknowledged), `finished` (writer closed), `path`, `stats`, `byte_limit`, `free_bytes`, `reserve_bytes`, `reason`, `error`, and a plain-English `message`. The existing top-level recording flag and counters remain. `recording_durable_stop: true` certifies these completion/error replies; automatic captures require it before preparation and again before starting, and refuse an older running daemon with platform-specific restart instructions. The CLI allows 35 s for a normal recording stop, while best-effort cleanup keeps its short deadline. `finished` alone does not mean success: a failed session also finishes; `error` must be absent. The status board shows the message.

Each normal recording has an 8 GiB ceiling; one with raw camera frames has 16 GiB. These allow long jig evaluations with headroom: observed ten-minute captures used about 313 MiB without pixels and 484 MiB with them. The 64 MiB ring, batches already removed from it and the file header all count toward the ceiling. The writer checks available disk space before the header and the first batch, then caches the allowance, deducting its writes. It refreshes after one second, after 16 MiB, or before the next batch could cross the reserve, keeping 5% of the filesystem's capacity free, clamped between 2 GiB and 20 GiB. The floor leaves room for other applications and is over thirty times the recording ring; the ceiling avoids reserving hundreds of GiB on a large disk. A refusal or space stop says how much is free and how much it keeps free. Concurrent disk users can still consume space between a check and a write; write and sync failures remain visible.

Batches end at record boundaries. At the space reserve the writer drops only unwritten whole records and syncs the prefix already saved; the size ceiling stops admission and drains what was accepted. Neither changes recording format v2. A stop caused by either policy returns `err` saying the recording ended early and whether its file is complete, so setup/calibration cannot silently fit a shortened capture. Recordings already on disk are never removed by this policy.
