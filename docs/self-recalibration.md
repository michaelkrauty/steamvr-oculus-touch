# Checking a sensor during play

An unseen controller is not evidence that a sensor moved. Each sensor is compared with the other sensors separately for each hand. Ordinary matched LEDs can establish agreement or disagreement. Missing sightings, including occlusion and a hand outside the image, can only request labels after two five-second windows. At most one native inspection or placement search runs at a time; cancellation, queued-work draining and restoration acknowledgement hold that slot until the search has stopped.

An inspection keeps the sensor's calibrated pose for ordinary tracking and requests cold correspondence search only for the suspect hands. Its deadline is thirty seconds. Each outstanding cold exposure retains its prior and peer confirmation until completion or cancellation; ordinary filter history remains 400 ms. A placement sample uses that held prior only once its exposure has left the history, and only if peers had confirmed the track then; any other sample waits for the IMU to pass its exposure. A copied cold candidate can track privately at frame rate while identifying itself. It cannot label shared blobs, supply a fast prior or export a pose before identity is established.

Identity uses relative rotation, independent of the disputed sensor calibration. Three exposures over at least a thirty-degree turn must follow the hand's IMU to within a tenth of the turn. When the other hand has IMU data, its turn must differ by at least half the requested hand's turn. Static, slow and indistinguishable motion remains unknown. An inspection that sees the controllers but cannot distinguish their motion retries after sixty seconds, doubling to ten minutes; other inconclusive inspections wait ten minutes. When another sensor is judged moved during an inspection, the inspection stops without an outcome, so no wait follows it. The movement verdict waits until that search's restoration is acknowledged and is then carried out, with no further window needed. Only a retraction drops it before then: an invalidation or overflow of any sensor's or hand's identities, since the window state it came from cannot be separated by epoch. The log says so once, and fresh windows judge the sensor again. Its sensor is never handed over while the verdict waits, so it is never released then.

## Correspondence lifetime and movement evidence

An identity follows at least five unchanged blob/LED pairs across consecutive raw frames. Each pair must also retain its private image lineage: every frame-to-frame step is at most 32 pixels, with no frame gap above 60 ms. Across ten captures, 3,162,942 pairs retaining the same recorded LED label had displacement median/p95/p99 of 0.156/6.458/13.459 pixels; 99.836% stayed within 32 pixels. Their maximum was 1120.882 pixels, including bad associations. Recorded labels are not physical-source truth. The 480,145 raw frame intervals had p99.9 of 22.14 ms and maximum 41.05 ms. Blobwatch's general nearest-neighbour tracking is unchanged.

A continuity break removes future permission to export that correspondence. Reacquisition gets a fresh motion proof. A normal end says nothing against already validated evidence. A retrospective invalidation, such as three consecutive tracked contradictions exceeding the greater of ten degrees and a tenth of the turn, removes that epoch's earlier checks and misses. Native events distinguish these reasons explicitly. Native code invalidates only the live epoch and clears it on any end, so an epoch that ended normally can never be retracted. Only invalidations are kept: starts and normal ends leave no lifecycle state, however long the engine runs. The invalidation mailbox is separate from the dropping camera-frame queue, and delayed frames cannot revive invalidated epochs. A tombstone outlives inspection counters and asynchronous restoration. The engine acknowledges a restored pose only after ending the sensor's identities and delivering every frame it tagged; the daemon then applies the last notices, drops the sensor's queued frames that carry a tombstoned identity and retires the tombstones. Its other queued frames wait for their ordinary pass, so the radio thread still handles at most one camera frame between controller reports. The movement and placement consumers receive only lifecycle changes, and a solver job that outlives its sensor's release is discarded. Each sensor and hand keeps at most 1,024 tombstones and counted epochs, not counting ordinary matching, so only a stalled consumer or a single search with more invalidations than that overflows. Overflow leaves a permanent watermark rejecting older evidence and clears affected residuals and movement streaks. Inspection frame tags use the former reserved words of the unchanged 64-byte device/248-byte frame record; additive recording type 9 carries the lifecycle event. Additive types 10 and 11 mark each engine open and each retirement: a replay keeps its tracking across a reopen, but forgets the old engine's identities there, as the daemon's new pipeline does, and retires a sensor's tombstones where the daemon did. Native epochs start again at one in each engine. Readers that do not know types 9 to 11 skip them. Shared memory is unchanged.

During one inspection, one hand must accumulate at least eighty eligible observed checks across at least two five-second windows, with at least sixty percent misses. Only windows with two other agreeing sensors on that same hand contribute. Epoch invalidation also removes a window if that epoch supplied all its checks. Normal ends retain them. The daemon's log line for a movement verdict gives this evidence for each deciding hand, or, for the two-window rule, the last window's observed checks with the residual and offset it was judged by. Movement and placement decisions drain pending lifecycle notices and commit under the mailbox lock. Tracking, solving, logging and pose application run outside that lock. A movement decision already applied is final. The small-bias consecutive-window rule, pixel thresholds and numerical placement fit criteria remain unchanged.

Requiring forty checks in each of two consecutive windows of an inspection fails on real cold-search timing, which produced adjacent windows such as 47 and 34 observed misses: ample disagreement, but no decision. Accumulating validated checks avoids discarding that evidence at a window boundary. It does not establish statistical independence or prove physical identity from software blob IDs.

## Derived state and its retraction

This table covers state derived from checks, probes, fits and window decisions, including state that can suppress future work. An invalidation is retrospective; a normal continuity end preserves validated evidence. Overflow treats every epoch at or below its watermark as invalidated. Epoch zero denotes ordinary matched tracking, outside inspection identity; it cannot be invalidated by an inspection notice. All final decisions are ordered against the native lifecycle mailbox. A notice already pending at that decision must be applied first. Later notices do not undo an already committed movement, placement or give-up decision.

Every derived state carries the hand and identity epoch it came from, failed placement attempts included, and lifecycle changes reach every consumer, also after the inspection that produced them has ended. State that depended on a peer is cleared together with it: a retracted sensor may have supplied the agreement that qualified another sensor's streak or inspection window. Only tombstones are lifecycle state; keeping normally ended epochs would make overflow, with its resets, the normal state of a long engine session.

## Remaining ambiguity and placement

A swap during an image overlap is caught only once the hands' rotations diverge; identical motion through an overlap can keep it briefly. The regression retains it through four seconds of identical rotation, and there is no fixed expiry while that motion remains indistinguishable. A one-pixel crossing with independent motion is revoked within three contradictory tracked exposures; its labels and private prior are removed. The [geometric-certificate follow-up](inspection-identity-recovery.md) addresses physical provenance and is not implemented here.

Placement probes retain their hand and epoch through the delayed-frame queue, gathering, saved confirmation fits and off-thread solver jobs. Invalidation discards affected probes and fits, including a completed result waiting in the solver channel; normal ends preserve them. One ambiguous exposure can enter a placement already running before identity revocation. The real placement regression injects the other controller's labels at 0.3 m separation and a 120-degree alias. A single exposure produces 0.172 mm error, compared with 0.176 mm without contamination. Four seconds of contamination can fit a pose 300 mm wrong, but the fresh confirmation batch disagrees or fails its label-share check; later clean batches are accepted within 0.100–0.134 mm. These tests exercise the existing minimum probe count, diversity, residual and two-batch agreement checks. They do not prove safety for indefinitely swapped, identically moving hands.

## Offline measurements

Recorded blob centres replace image extraction; inspection labels come from actual native correspondence/PnP searches at their measured CPU completion times. The pipeline retains its real history limits. The monitor runs at the daemon's one-second cadence. CPU pacing avoids treating host scheduling stalls as search work, but runs remain schedule-sensitive. Outside an artificially moved sensor's inspection, the harness strips its old recorded labels and does not regenerate ordinary native fast labels after restoration. These are offline tests on Linux, not a hardware calibration trial.

The harness holds each search's prior at its start, as the daemon does. A plain replay of a recording (`cv1touch track`) does not. It applies the recorded identity notices, engine opens and retirements, but recordings hold no search starts, and the replay runs no movement check. A search result that arrives more than 400 ms after its exposure, which the daemon judges from the prior held at search start, ends as too old in the replay, so its observed-LED counts can fall short of the daemon's.
